Fund manager role and BOI Small Cap: baseline research

A living baseline on what an Indian mutual-fund manager controls, a case study of Bank of India Small Cap Fund across three managers, and the thought patterns worth borrowing. Research only, not advice. First written 25 Nov 2026, IST.

How to read this page

  • Sections 1 and 2 are facts, each with its source address beside it.
  • Sections 3 to 5 are our analysis, labelled as such.
  • "Inferred" marks a conclusion we drew, not a fact we read. "Not found" means we searched and found no evidence.
  • Section 2's own return figures come from AMFI and mfapi daily prices (Direct Growth). They are tagged "Pending Fatail verification" until Fatail checks them.
  • This page holds no account details or personal holdings.

1. The role

Picture three rings. In the centre are the decisions the manager makes himself. Around them are the limits set by SEBI, the scheme document and his own firm. The outer ring holds what he does not control at all.

1.1 What the manager decides

  • Which stocks to buy, how much of each, when to buy and sell, and how much cash to hold, all inside the limits below.
  • Example from BOI: the team tracks about 1,000 stocks and keeps about 250 in an active list. It holds a stock while its reason for owning it holds. Source: https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/222103/alok-singh-boi-investment-mutual-fund-interview/

1.2 The rails

  • Category rule: a small-cap fund must keep at least 65% in small caps. Small caps are the 251st company onward by market value, on a list AMFI updates every six months. Source: https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2017/categorization-and-rationalization-of-mutual-fund-schemes_36199.html
  • Scheme document: BOI Small Cap may hold 65–100% in small caps and up to 35% in other stocks. Source: https://www.boimf.in/products/equity-funds/bank-of-india-small-cap-fund
  • Single-stock cap: usually no more than 10% of NAV in one company's shares (Seventh Schedule, 1996 Regulations). Source: https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2025/1741605370357.pdf
    • The 1996 Regulations have been replaced by the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026. We did not check whether the 10% wording carried over unchanged. Source: https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1792659909505.pdf
  • Liquidity stress tests: since Feb 2024, mid- and small-cap funds must publish each month how many days it would take to sell 25% and 50% of the portfolio. Sources: https://www.business-standard.com/markets/mutual-fund/mfs-told-to-disclose-stress-test-reports-of-midcap-smallcap-schemes-124022901229_1.html and https://www.amfiindia.com/risk-parameters
  • Investor-protection policy: AMCs keep a policy for mid- and small-cap investors (JM Financial's shown as an example). Source: https://www.jmfinancialmf.com/CMS/downloads/Statutory%20Disclosure/Policy%20to%20Protect%20the%20Interest%20of%20Investors%20of%20Mid%20Cap%20and%20Small%20Cap%20Schemes/Policy%20to%20Protect%20the%20Interest%20of%20Investors%20of%20Mid%20Cap%20and%20Small%20Cap%20Schemes.pdf
  • Pay held in fund units: at least 20% of a fund manager's eligible pay comes as units of the funds he oversees, locked for 3 years, and can be clawed back for misconduct. Sources: https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/apr-2021/1619614807999.pdf and https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/jun-2021/alignment-of-interest-of-key-employees-of-asset-management-companies-amcs-with-the-unitholders-of-the-mutual-fund-schemes_50693.html
  • Above him: the CIO, the AMC's investment and risk teams, the trustees and the board. Inferred: this is the standard structure. BOI's internal committee rules: not found. At BOI the CIO and the small-cap fund manager are the same person (see section 2).

1.3 What he does not control

  • Fees: the AMC sets the expense ratio within SEBI's caps. Since 1 Apr 2026 the cap for equity funds starts at 2.10% on the first ₹500 Cr and steps down as the fund grows. Source: https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/36520-sebi-cuts-expense-ratio-active-equity-funds-expense-ratio-now-ranges-between-095-and-210
  • BOI Small Cap Direct's base expense ratio is 0.44% (Oct 2026). Source: https://www.boimf.in/docs/default-source/investorcorner/factsheets/factsheet-october-2026.pdf
  • Investor money coming in and going out, marketing, and distributors.

1.4 The two engines of AUM growth

  1. Market gains: the portfolio's price (NAV) rises. This is the manager's skill.
  2. Net investor flows: more money comes in than goes out. The manager influences this only indirectly, because good recent returns draw money through distributors and ratings.

Size becomes a burden in small caps because a large fund cannot easily buy or sell thinly traded stocks. Nippon India Small Cap, at about ₹28,780 Cr, stopped taking lump sums from 7 Jul 2023 for that reason. Source: https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/nippon-india-smallcap-fund-stops-taking-lump-sum-subscription-from-july-7-fresh-sips-to-continue-with-limits-11688651914503.html

Manager versus process: BOI's CIO credits risk management and the house's process for results, not a single person. Source: https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/222103/alok-singh-boi-investment-mutual-fund-interview/

3. Thought patterns worth borrowing

Our analysis This section is our interpretation, not a source's claim and not advice.

3.1 How professionals turn a thesis into rules

  • Hold while the thesis holds: write the reason for owning something in one line (for example, "margins will rise"), check it on a schedule, and sell when the reason breaks, not when the price wobbles.
  • Cash-flow and ROE lenses: judge a business by whether it turns capital into cash at a good rate, the way a bond is judged by its yield.
  • PEG over P/E: a high P/E can be cheap if earnings grow fast. PEG divides P/E by earnings growth.
  • Liquidity and position size: never own more of something than you could exit calmly, and cap any single bet.
  • Check your own beta and drawdown: know how much your portfolio swings with the market and how deep its worst fall was, before the next fall arrives.

3.2 Vipul is not on the SEBI or RBI rails

A personal investor is not bound by fund-category rules, the 10% single-stock cap, stress-test disclosures, or a trustee board. That freedom cuts both ways.

4. Patterns to watch

Our analysis Drawn from the BOI case; not yet tested on other funds.
  • Manager-change timing: a new manager's absolute return mostly reflects where the market cycle stood on day one. Compare him with peers and the benchmark over the same dates.
  • Key-person risk: one person as CIO and manager of about 10 schemes. Both earlier small-cap managers left the AMC (2022 and 2024).
  • Style change after a handover: a fully replaced top 10 and a beta moving from 0.78 to 0.95 mean the old track record describes a different fund.
  • Inflow surge after a hot year: AUM doubled in seven months after a strong 2026 run. Fast inflows can water down future small-cap returns.

6. Open questions

  1. Question A (open since 19 Nov): after a manager change, when should we trust the fund's record again?
    • (a) From the current manager's start date: strict, and Fatail's current setting.
    • (b) From the longest-serving manager still on the team: Fatail's own pick.
    • (c) Restart only if the new manager really changed the holdings.
    • Inferred from this case: (a) misreads a timing effect as weakness, and (c) triggers here because the holdings did change. A peer- and benchmark-relative test of the current manager may be fairer, but only about 2 years of evidence exist.
